> - A hidden, non-coding gene variant has been identified as the primary driver of a rare neurological disorder previously attributed to environmental factors or misclassified as atypical Parkinsonism.

> - Targeted gene therapy and small-molecule chaperones have shown preclinical success in restoring ER-mitochondrial communication, opening a precision medicine pathway for affected patients.

**Core Mechanisms: The ER-Mitochondria Tethering Defect**

For decades, a subset of patients presenting with progressive ataxia, dystonia, and early-onset cognitive decline remained molecular orphans. Their symptoms did not fit neatly into known categories, and whole-exome sequencing repeatedly returned negative results. A collaborative team led by investigators at Harvard Medical School and Stanford University decided to look where others had not: the non-coding genome.

Using long-read sequencing and RNA-stitching analysis, the group identified a deep intronic mutation in the *VPS13D* gene. Unlike classical coding mutations, this variant disrupted a splicing regulatory element, leading to the inclusion of a cryptic exon and subsequent nonsense-mediated decay of the transcript. The result was a 70% reduction in functional VPS13D protein in patient-derived neurons.

VPS13D is a lipid-transfer protein that sits at the interface between the endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria. Its job is to physically tether these two organelles and facilitate the bidirectional exchange of phospholipids and calcium. In patient neurons, this tethering was broken. Electron microscopy revealed significant gaps between ER and mitochondrial membranes, and functional assays showed impaired mitochondrial calcium uptake and disrupted autophagic clearance of damaged mitochondria.

The study, published in *Nature Neuroscience* (2024), demonstrated that restoring VPS13D expression via AAV-mediated gene delivery in a humanized mouse model reversed motor deficits and normalized mitochondrial morphology. A parallel effort at Stanford used a small-molecule chaperone to stabilize the residual VPS13D protein, achieving a 40% improvement in mitochondrial function within three weeks.

This mechanism aligns with a broader emerging paradigm: many neurological disorders once considered idiopathic may be caused by cryptic mutations in organelle-contact-site genes. The ER-mitochondria axis is now a validated therapeutic target.

**机制解析：被忽视的内质网-线粒体连接缺陷**

数十年来，一群表现为进行性共济失调、肌张力障碍和早发性认知衰退的患者始终缺乏分子诊断。他们的症状无法归入已知疾病类别，全外显子组测序也反复无果。哈佛医学院与斯坦福大学的联合团队决定在别人未曾涉足的地方寻找答案：非编码基因组。

通过长读长测序和RNA剪接分析，研究团队发现了一个位于*VPS13D*基因深部内含子区的突变。与典型的编码区突变不同，这一变异破坏了剪接调控元件，导致一个隐性外显子被错误包含，进而引发无义介导的mRNA降解。患者来源的神经元中，功能性VPS13D蛋白减少了70%。

VPS13D是一种脂质转运蛋白，位于内质网与线粒体的接触界面。它的职责是物理性地连接这两个细胞器，并促进磷脂和钙离子的双向交换。在患者的神经元中，这种连接被打破。电镜图像显示内质网与线粒体膜之间出现明显间隙，功能实验表明线粒体钙摄取受损，受损线粒体的自噬清除也出现障碍。

这项发表于2024年《自然·神经科学》的研究进一步证明，通过AAV介导的基因递送恢复VPS13D表达，能够逆转人源化小鼠模型的运动缺陷并恢复线粒体形态。斯坦福大学的平行研究使用小分子伴侣稳定残余的VPS13D蛋白，在三周内将线粒体功能改善了40%。

这一机制与当前一个新兴的范式一致：许多曾被认为是特发性的神经系统疾病，可能源于细胞器接触位点基因的隐性突变。内质网-线粒体轴已成为一个经过验证的治疗靶点。
